### Notes:
- Finance is not like other sciences because is driven by people's behaviour (that is inherently irrational): hence, we cannot use the natural laws to deal with the market but instead we have to use the human laws.
- Hisotry never reapt itself. People always do. ~Voltaire
- People skills are more important than the technical side of money

- Our personal na dpast experiences (inflationary periods, financial crises, economical booms) deeply affect how we deal with money and life expectations
- In life, like in investing, one must be able to distinguish/decouple effort and outcome: fortune is always lurking around. Behind every successful story/personality there's always a big gamble. Don't obsess over single exceptional cases, focus instead on the trend/pattern and the general law.
- Your biggest enemies while accumulating wealth are ego and greed
- You don't need a tremendous effort to achieve a tremendous result: let the compounding effect work for you and remember that the secret ingredient of compounding is not the return rate but time!
- In order to stay wealthy you must avoiding loosing wealth: employ the "via negative"/inversion principle. Instead of thinking about becoming rich, try to avoid what would make you poor! (->connection to Shane Parrish and Nicholas Taleb, Jeff Bezos, Jacobi and Charlie Munger)
- Most of your portfolio performance will be decided during really short periods of chaos and panic.
- The financial genius is the person that is able to make rational choices while everyone around is going crazy.
- Don't underestimate tail probabilities: Everything depends how much you gain (resp loose) when you're right (resp wrong).
- Watchout for the fat tails behaviours! Few good stocks will generate most of your returns. Maximise your upward exposure while trying to cap your downward exposure (this is also the idea of PE). This is all really similar to the Price's Law (see also below)
- Accumulating wealth is all about gaining freedom, not money
- Most people want to be millionaire just to be able to spend money like millionaires to impress people (signalling theory); the truth is that people won't be impressed but will instead use your own ealth as their benchmark for their desire to be admired and respected. (No one is impressed with your own possessions as much as you are)
- Especially at the beginning of your saving journey, investment return is not as important as saving rate. 
- Saving (i.e., living below your means) must become a lifestyle, a mantra, a mindset
- Being cold rational comes with an enormous emotional/cognitive cost -> we are human!
- Aim to make reasonable choices, not cold rational
- Reality is too complex to try to predict the single event that will trigger a phase transition or a drastic change (-> chaos theory)
- tail events are impossible to foresee but will be extremely impactful (-> black swan)
- Catastrophic events are not an analysis failure but an imagination failure. 
- All extreme events are outcomes for which history didn't prepare us! like using an incomplete map. (Ironically we look behind while we decide where to go forward)
- Always account for a Risk Margin/Margin of Safety
- the best plans are the ones that account for things not to go according to the plan
- Easiest way to prepare for future problems is to watchout for single points of failure
- Don't overcommit to any strategy because you never know when the game rules or players behaviour will change
- Sunk cost will slow you down when you'll adjust your trajectory towards the new goal
- Everyone is playing with a different goal; don't compare your strategy with the one of others
- No one is crazy
- The asymetric aversion to loss is an evolutionary shoield ~Kahneman
- We're constantly surrounded by bad news because that;s the pattern/news/info we're biologically wired to recognize and be vigilant to. Newspapers are naturally exploiting this human trait. News is all about what happened and not what coul have happened.
- Markets adjust quickly even under extreme adverse scenarios; both the optimist and the pessimist will be wrong in the medium term: only the rational optimist won't be.
- Human beings are naturally attracted by a good storytelling
- The more we wisj something to be true the more we believe a story that overstates the odds of that to be true
- Humans have a collective good understanding of events and reality but individually no one has becaus ethe view is always incomplete. (-> Mental models, Charlie Munger) 
- We need to use several mental models at the same time so that different models cover the blind spots of the others
- We never learn from history because we like to fit the story that we like inside a much bigger and more complicated picture
